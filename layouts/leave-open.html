Leave it open

Empty space, on purpose. For the long coat, the laundry basket, the vacuum, or the machine that has to go somewhere.

Empty is a choice, not a gap

Every closet needs somewhere to put the thing that does not fit anywhere. A vacuum. A full length laundry basket. A suitcase. The ironing board. A dog crate.

Filling that space with shelves you then have to work around is the most common mistake in a closet design. Leaving a section open from the floor to the top shelf is deliberate, and it is what stops the floor of the rest of the closet filling up.

In a laundry room it is the machine

A laundry run is usually a counter over the washer and dryer, cabinets above, and one open section for the basket and the drying rack. The machines themselves sit in open space under the counter.

The builder treats that as an open piece, because that is what it is: a section with nothing in it, priced for the panels and the shelf and nothing else.

It is the cheapest piece

An open section costs the side panels it shares with the sections either side of it, plus its share of the top shelf. There is no hardware, no shelves and no front.

If a budget is tight, leaving one section open and adding shelves to it next year is a better answer than making every section narrower.

Before you ask

Questions about this piece

If yours is not here, the answer is a phone call away. Ask us directly.

Why would I pay for an empty section?

You are paying for the panels that define it and the shelf over it, which is what makes it a tidy opening rather than a gap. It is the cheapest piece in the system.

Can I add shelves to it later?

Yes. The panels either side are already drilled the full height at 32mm centres, so shelves and pins drop straight in.

Does an open section still get a top shelf?

Yes, the top shelf runs across the whole system at 84 inches.

Is this where the washer and dryer go?

In a laundry room, yes. The machines sit in open sections under the counter.

How wide should I leave it?

Measure the thing going in it. A vacuum wants about 16, a laundry basket about 24, a full size machine about 28.
