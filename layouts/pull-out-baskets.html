Pull-out baskets

Chrome wire on full-extension glides, so you can see the back of the basket without unloading the front.

Why wire, and why pull out

A basket on full extension glides comes all the way out of the carcass, so you look down into it instead of reaching into the dark. On a fixed shelf the back third of the depth is effectively lost.

Wire breathes, which matters for gym kit and swimwear, and you can see through it from above, which matters for everything else.

What goes in them

Anything you dig through rather than fold. Gym clothes, swimwear, scarves, hats, kids clothes, dog things.

Not shirts. A folded shirt in a wire basket comes out creased across the wires. Folded goes on a shelf or in a drawer.

How we build it

One to six baskets in a section, from a 10 inch block up to 48 inches. Three is the usual.

Full extension glides, so the basket clears the front of the carcass completely.

Shelving over the top as standard, because the baskets are low and the wall above them is free. A section that is baskets floor to ceiling wastes the easiest storage in the closet.

Before you ask

Questions about this layout

If yours is not here, the answer is a phone call away. Ask us directly.

How many baskets fit in a section?

One to six, in a block from 10 to 48 inches. Three is the usual, with shelving above.

Why not just use shelves?

On a fixed shelf the back third of the depth is effectively lost because you cannot see into it. A basket on full extension glides comes right out.

Will clothes crease in them?

Folded shirts will, across the wires. Baskets are for things you dig through, not things you fold. Folded goes on a shelf or in a drawer.

Can I have baskets floor to ceiling?

You can, but it wastes the easiest storage in the closet. We put shelving over them as standard.

Are the baskets chrome?

Yes, chrome wire on full extension glides. The finish is listed on the parts list with everything else.
