Every layout we build

A closet is not one thing. It is a row of sections, each doing one job. These are the ten we build, and the real heights and spans each one needs.

Build yours in 3D Or book a free measure →

How to read this page

Pick the sections, not the closet. Almost every closet we install is three to six of these side by side.

A section is one vertical slice of the run, 24 to 36 inches wide, with its own job. A wall of identical double hanging is a cheap closet. A wall of three different sections, chosen for what you actually own, is a good one.

Each page below gives the real numbers: the clear height that layout needs, the depth it wants, and the combinations it rules out. Those are the same numbers the 3D builder enforces, which is why it will not let you draw a double hang over a drawer bank.

Double Hanging

Two rods stacked in one section, at 80 inches and 40 inches. Shirts and jackets up top, folded trousers below.

Long Hanging

One rod at 66 inches with clear floor under it. Dresses, coats, robes, anything long.

Hanging + Shelves

A rod below for what hangs, two adjustable shelves above for what folds.

Adjustable Shelves

Up to ten shelves in a section, on 32mm pin centres, so the spacing you choose now is not the spacing you are stuck with.

Drawer Tower

A bank of soft-close drawers with open shelving over it. The piece that stops a closet being only rails.

Hanging + Drawers

Four drawers at the bottom, a rod above them. The section most bedroom closets want two of.

Pull-Out Baskets

Chrome wire on full-extension glides, so you can see the back of the basket without unloading the front.

Shoe Wall

Angled shelves with a front fence, the full height of the section. You see the shoe, not the toe.

Hamper + Hanging

A tilt-down basket at the bottom, a rod above it. The pile on the floor gets somewhere to go.

Cubbies

A grid of open compartments. Nothing to open, everything visible.

Hanging

One rod. On its own it hangs full length for coats and dresses; over drawers or a hamper it shortens to suit; a rod in both halves is a double hang.
