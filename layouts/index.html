Every piece we build with

A closet is not one thing. It is a row of sections, each doing one job. These are the eleven pieces a section is built from, and which half of a section each one goes in.

How to read this page

Pick the sections, not the closet. Almost every closet we install is three to six of these side by side.

A section is one vertical slice of the run, 24 to 36 inches wide, with its own job. A wall of identical hanging is a cheap closet. A wall of three different sections, chosen for what you actually own, is a good one.

Each page below is one piece: what it is, which half of a section it goes in, and the real numbers it needs. Those are the same numbers the 3D builder enforces, which is why it will not let you draw a double hang over a drawer bank.

Hanging

One rod. Where it sits in the section is what decides whether your clothes hang full length, hang short over drawers, or hang on two levels.

Adjustable shelves

Shelves that move. Drilled on 32mm centres the full height of the panel, so the spacing you pick today is not the spacing you are stuck with.

Cubbies

A grid of open compartments. Each one holds a bag upright, a stack of folded knits or a hat, and nothing leans on anything else.

Shoe shelving

Flat shelves at 8 inch spacing. The cheapest way to get every pair off the floor and in a row you can see along.

Slanted shoe shelves

Angled shelves with a rail along the front. The shoe tips toward you, so you see the whole thing rather than its toe.

Leave it open

Empty space, on purpose. For the long coat, the laundry basket, the vacuum, or the machine that has to go somewhere.

Drawers

A bank of soft-close drawers at the bottom of a section. The piece that stops a closet being only rails and shelves.

Pull-out baskets

Chrome wire baskets on full-extension runners. You see what is at the back without unloading what is at the front.

Pull-out shelves

A solid shelf on full-extension glides. It comes out to you, so the back of a deep run is as usable as the front.

Tilt-out hamper

A basket behind a front that tips out toward you. Laundry off the floor, out of sight, and in the closet rather than on the landing.

Closed door

A slab front across the whole section, from the toe kick to the top shelf. Dust out, mess hidden, and a flat wall where you want one.

Glass door

A framed glass front across the whole section. The dust stays out and you can still see what is in there without opening it.
