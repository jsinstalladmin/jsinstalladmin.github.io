Cubbies

A grid of open compartments. Nothing to open, everything visible.

Open, and visible

A grid of compartments with nothing to open. You see everything at once, which is the whole point, and there is no door or drawer front to pay for.

They suit handbags, hats, helmets, folded knits and sports kit. Anything that has a shape and should keep it.

How they are built

The vertical dividers are fixed, not adjustable, because they carry the shelf over them. That is what makes a cubby grid rigid enough to take a loaded bag without racking.

The whole block is capped with a fixed top shelf. Every exposed edge is banded.

Compartment size is set by what goes in it. A handbag cubby is usually 12 to 14 inches wide and 14 tall. A hat cubby is wider and shallower.

Where they go wrong

A whole wall of cubbies looks wonderful empty and chaotic full. They work best as a block inside a run, usually at eye level, with hanging or drawers around them.

They are also open, which means dust. In a dusty house, a drawer or a door is the better answer for anything you do not touch weekly.

Before you ask

Questions about this layout

If yours is not here, the answer is a phone call away. Ask us directly.

What size should a cubby be?

Set by what goes in it. A handbag cubby is usually 12 to 14 inches wide and 14 tall. Hats want wider and shallower.

Can the dividers move?

No. They are fixed, because they carry the shelf over them. That is what keeps a loaded cubby grid from racking.

Should I do a whole wall of them?

We would not. They look wonderful empty and chaotic full. A block at eye level inside a run of hanging and drawers is where they earn their place.

Do they collect dust?

Yes, they are open. For anything you do not touch weekly, a drawer or a door is the better answer.

Is the top capped?

Always, with a fixed shelf, and every exposed edge is banded.
