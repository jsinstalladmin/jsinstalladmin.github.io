Closet installation

The old shelving comes out, the new system goes in, and the van leaves with the packaging. Usually one day, by the people who measured the room.

The day itself

Most closets are a single day. Larger walk-ins and built-ins run into a second.

We arrive with the cabinetry already cut to your room, not to a catalogue size. The old wire shelving and its brackets come out first, and the holes they leave get filled. Then the cleat goes on, laser-levelled, and the run hangs off it.

Every partition is plumbed, every shelf levelled, every drawer and door adjusted until the gaps between fronts are even. A drawer that catches on the way out is not finished, so it does not get signed off.

We take the offcuts and the packaging with us, patch what we drilled, and vacuum the floor. The one thing we leave behind is the closet.

What we work around

Real rooms are not square, and nothing about a house is where a drawing says it is. That is why the measure takes forty minutes instead of five.

Walls that run out of plumb get scribed to. Baseboard either gets cut around or the run gets packed off it. An outlet or a light switch inside the closet stays reachable, which means the section in front of it stays open at the back. Baseboard heat means the whole run goes wall hung, clear of the element.

A window caps the cabinetry under it at the sill. A sloped ceiling caps it at the low point. A column takes its own width off the wall. All of that is settled on the drawing before anything is cut, which is why installation day is quiet.

You can see the same rules working in the 3D builder: mark what is on your wall and the layout and the price both move.

Mounted off the floor, or on it

Two ways to hang a closet, and the choice is not only about looks.

Wall hung means the whole run carries on a cleat screwed into the studs, with nothing touching the floor. You can vacuum straight under it, the floor reads as one surface, and it is the only option over baseboard heat.

Floor standing sits on a toe kick. It is the stronger of the two for very heavy loads, which is why garages are usually built this way, and it hides an uneven floor better.

Either way the load goes into framing, not into drywall anchors. A closet full of winter coats is heavier than people expect.

Before you ask

Questions we get about this

If yours is not here, the answer is a phone call away. Ask us directly.

How long does an installation take?

Most closets are one day. A large walk-in with an island, or several rooms at once, runs into a second day. We tell you which it is when we quote, not on the morning.

Do you take the old shelving out?

Yes. Removing the old wire shelving and brackets, and filling the holes they leave, is part of the install. You do not need to strip the closet before we arrive.

Does it damage the walls?

We drill into studs for the cleat and patch anything we open up. Nothing is glued to the drywall and nothing is cut into it. If a wall needs repainting afterwards it is because the old shelving was there a long time, not because of us.

Do I need to be home?

Someone over eighteen needs to be there to let the crew in and to look at it at the end. You do not need to hover. Most people work from another room.

What if something is wrong on the day?

It gets fixed. The crew carries spare hardware and a track saw, so a shelf that needs trimming or a front that needs rehanging is dealt with before they leave rather than booked in for another visit.
