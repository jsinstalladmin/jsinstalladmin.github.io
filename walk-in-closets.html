Walk-In Closets

A walk-in is the one room in the house where every inch is yours to assign. We measure the room, lay out the hanging, and build the cabinetry to the dimensions we took — not to a catalogue size that nearly fits.

Single wall · Minimum 5'0" wideL-shape · Minimum 6'6" × 7'0"U-shape · Minimum 7'6" wide

Built to the room,
not to a catalogue

Most walk-in closets waste their best wall. Builders hang a single rod at 66 inches and leave four feet of dead air above it and two feet of dead floor below. Doubling that wall into an 80-inch and 40-inch run alone gives you back roughly twice the hanging in the same footprint.

We lay a walk-in out in three passes. First the long hang — coats, dresses, anything over 60 inches — which gets one wall and a 66-inch rod. Then the double hang for shirts, folded trousers and jackets, which takes the bulk of the room at 80 and 40 inches. Last the fixed storage: drawers at waist height where you actually reach into them, shoes on 8-inch spacing, and a top shelf at 84 inches for the things you touch twice a year.

Questions

How long does a walk-in closet take to install?
Most walk-ins are installed in a single day. Larger island layouts occasionally run into a second morning. Manufacturing lead time before that is typically two to three weeks depending on the finish you choose.
Do you have to damage the walls?
No. The system hangs off a rail fixed into studs, and the vertical panels carry the load. We patch the few screw holes we make. There is no demolition and no drywall work.
Can I move the shelves later?
Yes. Adjustable shelves sit on 5mm pins drilled at 32mm centres down the full height of each panel, so you can move any adjustable shelf in one-and-a-quarter-inch steps without drilling anything new.
What is the widest a shelf can span?
Thirty-six inches. Past that, three-quarter-inch melamine sags under load over time. If a section needs to be wider than 36 inches we put a partition in it, which is why section widths on our drawings are always between 24 and 36 inches.
Do you build around windows, angled ceilings and air returns?
Yes — that is the whole point of building to measure. Baseboard notches, radius and pie corner shelves, staggered and partial panels and filler panels all exist for exactly these situations.
