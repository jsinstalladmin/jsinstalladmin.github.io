Adjustable means adjustable

Shelves sit on pins at 32mm centres, top to bottom, both sides. Moving one is a two minute job with no tools and no new holes, because the holes are already there.

That matters more than it sounds. What you store changes. The spacing you choose on the day of the measure is almost never the spacing you want two years later.

How many, and how far apart

Folded shirts and knits: 12 to 14 inches apart. Any more and the stack topples, any less and you cannot get a hand in.

Bins and baskets: measure the bin, add two inches.

Handbags: 14 to 18 inches, and a divider between them so they stand up.

Shoes on a flat shelf: 8 inches. Boots: 16 to 18.

The 36 inch rule

A three quarter inch melamine shelf will carry a normal closet load across 36 inches without visible deflection. Past that it bows, and once melamine bows it stays bowed.

So a 48 inch opening does not get a 48 inch shelf. It gets a partition and two shelves, which is also stronger, also looks better, and costs less than the alternative of a thicker shelf or a steel stiffener.

Garage shelving is built to shorter spans again, because the load is paint cans and tools rather than jumpers.

Before you ask

Questions about this layout

If yours is not here, the answer is a phone call away. Ask us directly.

How far apart should shelves be?

Twelve to fourteen inches for folded clothes, eight for shoes, sixteen to eighteen for boots and handbags, and the height of the bin plus two inches for bins.

Can I really move them?

Yes, by hand, in about two minutes. They sit on pins at 32mm centres and the holes are already drilled top to bottom.

How many shelves can I have in one section?

Up to ten. Most sections take four to six.

Why is 36 inches the maximum span?

Past that a three quarter inch melamine shelf deflects under a real load, and melamine does not spring back. A partition is stronger, looks better and costs less than the alternatives.

What are they made of?

Three quarter inch thermally fused laminate, the same board as the carcass, banded on every exposed edge.
