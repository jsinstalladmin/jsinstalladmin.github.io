Pantry Systems

A pantry fails for one reason: the shelves are too deep and too far apart, so everything behind the front row disappears.

Canned goods · 8"–10" spacing, 12" deepBoxed goods · 14"–16" spacing, 14" deepSmall appliances · 18"–20" spacing, 16" deep

Built to the room,
not to a catalogue

Fourteen to sixteen inches is the right depth for a pantry shelf. Past that, cans sit in a second row you cannot see and will not use. We build pantry shelving shallow and close together, which sounds like less storage and is in practice a great deal more.

Shelf spacing matters as much as depth. Cans and jars want 8 to 10 inches. Cereal boxes and small appliances want 14 to 16. Because every shelf sits on 5mm pins at 32mm centres, you can change your mind about any of it later without new holes.

Questions

How deep should pantry shelves be?
Twelve to sixteen inches. Deeper than that and the back row is invisible, which is the single most common complaint about builder-grade pantries.
Can you build around the door swing?
Yes. We measure the swing before we draw, and filler panels handle the strike side so nothing fouls.
Can you put lighting in?
On fixed shelves, yes — there is a fixed-shelf-with-strip part made for it. Adjustable shelves cannot carry a wired strip because they move.
What finish holds up to a pantry?
Any of the 78. They are all thermally fused laminate over a three-quarter-inch core, which wipes clean and does not absorb spills the way a painted wood shelf does.
