Why it fits

Closet storage built around your space and the way you actually live

A closet works when it is planned for the people using it. A shared primary closet needs two wardrobes kept apart. A small apartment closet has to use every inch of ceiling height. A coastal house needs materials that survive humidity. A garage needs shelves that will not bow under paint tins.

We start every job with those details rather than with a catalogue layout, which is why the drawing you approve and the cabinetry that turns up are the same thing.

What we build

Walk-In Closets

A walk-in is the one room in the house where every inch is yours to assign. We measure the room, lay out the hanging, and build the cabinetry to the dimensions we took, not to a catalogue size that nearly fits.

Reach-In Closets

The standard builder's closet is one rod and one shelf, and it wastes more than half the box. A reach-in system is the cheapest square-foot upgrade in the house.

Pantry Systems

A pantry fails for one reason: the shelves are too deep and too far apart, so everything behind the front row disappears.

Garage Storage

A garage gets taken over from the floor up. The fix is to get everything onto the wall and leave the slab clear.

Laundry Rooms

The process

How a closet actually gets built

Four steps, about three weeks end to end, and you know the price before anything is ordered.

1

Consultation and measure

Free, in your home, about forty minutes. We take the room, the baseboard, the door swing, the outlets and anything else in the way, and we talk through what has to fit in it.

2

Design and 3D drawing

A real elevation with a fixed price, and a 3D view of the finished room. Revisions until it is right. No deposit until you are happy with it.

3

Fabrication

Two to three weeks for most colors. Every panel is cut to the dimensions we took on the day, on an industrial line, and edge banded on every exposed edge.

4

Professional installation

Usually one day. We mount, level and adjust every component, take the packaging away with us and patch what we drilled.

Before you ask

The eight questions we get every week

If yours is not here, the answer is a phone call away. Ask us directly.

What does a custom closet actually cost?

There is no single answer, because the price comes off linear feet, the color you pick, and how many drawers and doors are in it. Rather than give you a range that means nothing, the 3D builder prices your own room section by section off the same parts list we order from, and this guide breaks down what moves the number.

Can I get a price without anyone coming to the house?

Yes. Put your room’s real width, depth and height into the builder, lay out the inside, and the total updates as you go. Nothing is hidden behind a form and nobody calls you to release the number.

Do you charge for the measure?

No. The measure is free and there is no obligation attached to it. We bring a tape and a laser, measure the room properly, and you get the drawing and the price whether you go ahead or not.

How long does the whole thing take?

The install itself is usually one day. Before that, the lead time is set by the color you choose: the stocked whites and greys run about a week, most woodgrains two to four weeks, and a handful of specialty colors five to eight. Every one of the 79 colors shows its own lead time on its tile.

Will the people who measure it be the people who install it?

Yes. The same crew measures, builds and installs. No subcontractors are sent to your house, which is the whole reason the measurement and the finished cabinetry match.

What is the cabinetry actually made of?

¾" thermally fused laminate on a furniture-grade core, which is the same material commercial casework is built from, edge-banded on every exposed edge. This guide explains why that outlasts plywood and wire in a closet, and where it does not.

How wide can a shelf be before it bows?

Past about 36 inches a ¾" shelf deflects visibly under a normal load, so a partition goes in instead. That is why the builder will not let you draw a section wider than 36 inches. The 36-inch rule, in full.

Which areas do you cover?

Five regions, each with its own crew and its own schedule: South Florida, New Jersey, New York, Connecticut and Pennsylvania. The map further up this page lists the counties and towns in each. If you are just outside one of them, ask anyway.
