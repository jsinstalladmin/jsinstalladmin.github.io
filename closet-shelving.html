Closet shelving and organisers

Shelves cut to your room and spaced to what goes on them, with the drawers, baskets and rails that keep the small things findable.

Book a free measure Or design yours in 3D →

Shelving that is cut, not bought

A shelf is only as useful as its span and its depth. Both are decided by what you put on it.

Every shelf is cut from the same ¾" thermally fused laminate as the carcass and edge banded on every exposed edge, so it does not swell where hands touch it. It sits on 32mm pin centres, which means you can move it later without drilling anything.

Depth is chosen per run. 14" takes folded knits and handbags without swallowing them. 16" takes bins and linen. A 24" hanging section is a different depth again, which is why one shelf depth across a whole closet is a compromise rather than a design.

Past about 36 inches of clear span a ¾" shelf deflects under a real load, so a partition goes in instead. That rule in full.

The organisers that earn their place

Not everything sold as a closet accessory is worth paying for. These are the ones we fit again and again.

Angled shoe shelving shows the whole shoe instead of a row of toes, and fits more pairs per foot than a flat shelf because the heels nest.

Pull-out wire baskets on full-extension glides for anything you dig through: gym kit, swimwear, kids' clothes. You can see the back of the basket without unloading the front.

Drawer towers with soft-close undermount glides, divided for watches, belts and jewellery. The divider matters more than the drawer.

A tilt-out hamper under a hanging rod, so the pile on the floor has somewhere to be.

Valet rods and belt and tie racks, which cost almost nothing and are the things people say they use most.

Built in, or pre-built

Two ways to buy the same cabinetry, and the difference is money and lead time rather than quality.

Built in means every panel is cut to your room: wall to wall, floor to ceiling, scribed to what is already there. It looks like part of the house rather than furniture pushed against a wall. This is what most of our work is.

Pre-built uses standard module widths configured to fit. It is quicker and cheaper, and it is the right answer in a rental, in a room you will not keep, or when the budget has to go on the hanging rather than the carcass.

You can price both yourself: put your room into the 3D builder and change the section widths. Standard widths are the ones it offers.

Before you ask

Questions we get about this

If yours is not here, the answer is a phone call away. Ask us directly.

Can I move the shelves later?

Yes. They sit on pins at 32mm centres, so a shelf moves up or down without tools and without drilling. Fixed shelves are only used where one is doing structural work.

How much weight will a shelf take?

Within the 36 inch span we build to, a ¾" shelf carries a normal closet load without visible deflection. Garage shelving is built to shorter spans and deeper sections for the same reason.

Can you match shelving to a closet I already have?

Sometimes. If it is a system from a major manufacturer we can usually work alongside it, but matching the decor exactly is rarely possible because board decors are discontinued. We will tell you honestly at the measure rather than guess.

Are the accessories extra?

They are itemised, so you can see what each one costs and take it off. The builder prices them as you add them, off the same parts list we order from.

What finishes can the shelving be?

All 79. Shelving is usually the carcass finish; drawer and door fronts can be a different one. See the whole library.
